Council adopted the new Road Maintenance Service Levels at the August
meeting, so the pothole system needs to know about priorities and
response times. Rosalind has the adopted document if you want it, but this
is what matters for us.

**Priority**

Every report gets a priority when a crew leader or I assess it. Until it's
assessed it shows as "Not yet assessed", and it must not look like low
priority. The priorities:

- **P1 – Make safe**: a hazard to traffic. Deeper than 100 mm, or wider
  than 300 mm on a road signed 80 km/h or more, or anywhere on a school bus
  route regardless of size. Must be made safe (signed, coned or filled)
  within 24 hours of the report coming in.
- **P2 – Repair soon**: 50–100 mm deep on a sealed road. Repaired within
  10 working days.
- **P3 – Programmed**: anything smaller on a sealed road. Repaired within
  60 days, normally as part of a programmed patching run.
- **Gravel roads** do not get P2 or P3. Either P1 or "Grading program",
  which has no deadline on this system.

**Clock rules**

- The clock starts when the report is received, not when it's assessed.
  If a report sits unassessed for two days and then turns out to be P1,
  it is already overdue. That's the point, I want to see that.
- Working days are Monday to Friday excluding public holidays and the
  Christmas shutdown (we close from 24 December to the first Monday after
  1 January). P1's 24 hours is real hours, weekends included. We're on
  call.
- A P1 is "met" when it is made safe within 24 hours, even if the actual
  repair comes later. So a P1 needs two dates: made safe, and repaired.
  After it's made safe it becomes a P2 for the repair, with the 10 working
  days counted from the made-safe date.
- Duplicates (from the storm change) take the priority and due date of
  the main report. A duplicate never makes the main report's clock start
  later. If a duplicate came in *earlier* than the one we picked as main,
  the clock starts from the earliest one.
- "Not ours" reports don't have a due date at all.

**Worked examples**

- Report in Monday 9:00, assessed Monday 14:00 as P2. Due end of day
  Monday two weeks later (10 working days). If the second Monday is a
  public holiday, due the Tuesday.
- Report in Friday 16:30, assessed Monday 8:00 as P1. Due Saturday 16:30.
  Already overdue on assessment. Show it red.
- Report in 20 December, P2. Shutdown means working days stop on the 23rd
  and restart on the first Monday in January.
- School bus route, 40 mm deep: P1, regardless of depth.

**What I need to see**

- Priority and due date on the list and on the map pin (colour by
  priority, a ring or something when overdue).
- A monthly figure for the council report: for each priority, how many
  were due that month and what percentage were met on time. Unassessed
  ones that went past 24 hours count as missed P1s until assessed, because
  we can't say they weren't P1s.
- Which roads are on the school bus routes: I'll send the list of streets
  from the transport contractor. For now let crew leaders tick a "school
  bus route" box on the report.

Don't let anyone backdate the made-safe or repaired dates by more than
7 days without a note saying why. Auditors asked about this last year.
