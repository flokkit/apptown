Now that the papers are in one place, they need to follow the records
retention schedule properly. This is the part that most worries me
before the state records audit in May.

**What we keep and for how long**

| Record                                         | Keep for                        |
|------------------------------------------------|---------------------------------|
| Agendas and attachments (public)               | Permanently                     |
| Confirmed minutes                              | Permanently                     |
| Unconfirmed (draft) minutes                    | Until confirmed, then 2 years   |
| Confidential attachments                       | 15 years after the meeting      |
| Audit and risk committee papers                | 10 years after the meeting      |
| Superseded versions of any document            | Same as the document it replaced|
| Audio recordings of meetings                   | 12 months after the minutes are confirmed |

"Permanently" means it's transferred to the state archive after 25
years, not deleted, but that's a manual process and not for now.

**Disposal**

- When something reaches the end of its retention period, it goes on a
  "due for disposal" list. It is not deleted automatically.
- I review the list and approve disposal, item by item or in a batch,
  with a disposal authority number (like "GDA-10 class 3.2.1").
- When it's disposed of, the file is removed, but a record stays: what it
  was, the meeting, the retention class, when it was disposed of, who
  approved it, and the authority number. That record is kept permanently.
- Nothing can be disposed of while it's on legal hold. I need to be able
  to put a meeting, or a single document, on hold with a reason, and only
  I or the CEO can take it off.

**Confidential items becoming public**

- Each confidential item can have a release date or a release condition
  ("on completion of the contract"). When the date comes, or when I mark
  the condition met, it goes on a list for me to approve release. It
  isn't released automatically.
- Once released, it shows on the public page with a note "Released
  [date], previously confidential under section ..." and keeps its
  confidential-era history.
- A released item's retention changes to the public rule (permanent).

**Worked examples**

1. Draft minutes of the 14 March 2024 meeting, confirmed 11 April 2024.
   Due for disposal 11 April 2026. The confirmed minutes are permanent.
2. Confidential attachment from 9 June 2011 (a land sale). Due for
   disposal 9 June 2026, unless it's on hold or was released.
3. The 2019 Main Street land acquisition papers are on legal hold
   because of the ongoing dispute. They never appear on the due list
   while the hold is on, even if they're past their date.
4. An agenda from 2022 that was replaced because the wrong attachment
   was uploaded. The superseded version is permanent too, like the
   agenda it belonged to.
5. Audio from the 8 August meeting, minutes confirmed 12 September: due
   12 September next year.

If anything is unclear about which class a document is in, show it as
"not classified" and don't put it on any disposal list until someone
classifies it.
