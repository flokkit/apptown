# Coldwater Shire Council

A regional local government covering about 19,000 people across a wide,
mostly farming district. The council offices are in Coldwater, the largest
town; the other towns are Harrow Flat, Tumby Creek, Ellisford and the
village of Marrowbank. There are nine elected councillors, a CEO, and about
210 staff, most of them outdoors at the works depot, the transfer station
and the parks crews. The software team is three people under the IT lead,
and they look after everything from the phone system to the website.

Council has a long memory and a short budget. Residents ring the front
counter when something breaks, councillors hear about it at the bakery, and
the local paper reads every agenda. Anything published can end up quoted
back at a council meeting.

## How we work

These are the house rules. They apply to every app, including ones built
before the rule was written down. Some of them are tightened by later
requests (the `rule` requests), which say so.

1. **Public pages must be accessible.** Anything residents can see meets
   WCAG 2.2 AA: it works with a keyboard and a screen reader, has enough
   colour contrast, and has alt text on images. (At the start this is
   "try to remember"; the website's accessibility request makes it a
   checked gate.)
2. **Official records need the governance officer's sign-off.** Agendas,
   minutes and anything else council publishes as a formal record is not
   published until Rosalind Achebe (or her delegate
   when she is away) has approved that exact version.
3. **Residents' personal details never appear on public pages.** No names,
   phone numbers, email addresses or home addresses of residents who
   report, submit, book or register. Applicants on planning notices are
   shown by their company name or as "Private applicant". Street names
   are fine; house numbers of complainants are not.
4. **Keep records for as long as the retention schedule says, and no
   longer.** Records are kept according to the council's retention
   schedule (Rosalind holds it). Nothing in an official record is deleted
   by hand; disposal is a recorded act.
5. **Plain English.** Write for a resident reading on a phone in a ute.
   No council jargon on public pages without explaining it.
6. **Changes to how systems run go through IT.** New servers, schedules,
   backups, outside services or anything that costs money each month is
   approved by Marcus Teo.
7. **Caretaker period.** In the weeks before a council election, nothing
   published may promote a councillor or candidate, and new council
   announcements need the CEO's office to agree. Rosalind sets the dates.
