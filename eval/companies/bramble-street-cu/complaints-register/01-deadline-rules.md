Here are the complaint deadline rules. Rosa and I have checked these against
our complaints policy. If something here is ambiguous, please ask rather
than pick.

## When a complaint is received

- The received time is when it first reached any staff member, not when it
  was logged. Staff type in the actual time.
- If it arrives after 5pm, or on a day that isn't a business day, it counts
  as received at 9am on the next business day. Business days are Monday to
  Friday excluding our public holiday list (house rule 7).

## Deadlines

| Step | Deadline |
|------|----------|
| Acknowledge | by the end of the next business day after received |
| Final response, standard complaint | 30 calendar days after received |
| Final response, complaint about hardship, a default notice or collections | 21 calendar days after received |
| Early resolution | if resolved to the person's satisfaction within 5 business days, it can be closed without a written final response. It is still recorded. |

If a final response deadline falls on a non-business day, it moves to the
next business day.

## Stopping the clock

When we ask the complainant for information we need, and say so in writing,
the final response clock stops on the day we ask and restarts on the day we
receive it. Stopped days don't count. Only the complaints team can stop the
clock and they must record what was asked. The clock can be stopped more
than once. Acknowledgement isn't affected.

## Delay notices

If we can't meet the final response deadline, we must send a delay notice
before the deadline, giving the reasons and telling the person they can go
to the external complaints scheme now. Record it. Sending a delay notice
does not move the deadline; the complaint shows as overdue from the
deadline anyway, but "overdue with notice" and "overdue without notice"
are reported separately.

## Changing category

If a complaint is recategorised to or from hardship, the deadline is
recalculated from the original received date. If that makes it already
overdue, show that.

## Worked examples

(Dates are in 2026. Assume no public holidays unless stated.)

1. Phone complaint at 10:20am Tuesday 3 March. Acknowledge by end of
   Wednesday 4 March. Standard final response: Thursday 2 April.
2. Email at 6:45pm Friday 6 March. Counts as 9am Monday 9 March.
   Acknowledge by end of Tuesday 10 March. Final response: Wednesday 8 April.
3. Letter opened Saturday 14 March (someone was in doing the post). Counts
   as Monday 16 March. Hardship complaint, so final response Monday 6 April.
   If 6 April is a public holiday (Easter Monday that year), it moves to
   Tuesday 7 April.
4. Standard complaint received Monday 2 March. On Monday 9 March we ask for
   statements. They arrive Monday 16 March. Seven days stopped. Deadline
   moves from Wednesday 1 April to Wednesday 8 April.
5. Fee complaint received Monday 2 March, resolved by refund on Thursday
   5 March and the member says that's fine. Early resolution, no final
   response letter needed.
6. Complaint received Monday 2 March as "service", recategorised to
   "hardship" on Tuesday 24 March. 21 days from 2 March is Monday 23 March,
   so it's already overdue, and it shows "overdue without notice".

## What the register shows

For each open complaint: days remaining (or days overdue) to the next
deadline, whether the clock is stopped, and a status of on track, due in
5 days or fewer, overdue with notice, overdue without notice.
